## Her points, paraphrased (short originals in quotes)

1. **Confounds in CBT effect studies.** Many confounds, above all therapy time and contact: venting ("desahogarse, ventilarse") helps regardless of technique. "Hay muchos confounds, sobre todo el tiempo de la terapia."
   - Maps to our R-section on non-specific factors / active-ingredient questions; to test against attention-control and time-matched comparator trials.
2. **Strategic therapy: the "attempted solution" is the problem.** What the person does to solve the problem perpetuates it; "la solución se vuelve el problema". Example: with insomnia, trying to force sleep keeps it away ("como un gato negro: entre más lo persigues, más se va"). Strategic therapy works by interrupting the attempted solution.
   - Candidate link: paradoxical-intention / sleep-effort literature (to be found and verified; nothing cited here).
3. **Reason does not change behaviour; emotion does.** "Perversión de la razón": people already know what to eat or that a phobic fear is improbable, and still act otherwise. Strategic therapy works with four emotions: horror, pleasure, fear, rage ("horror, placer, miedo y rabia"). Her examples: (a) smoking is driven by pleasure, and graphic warning images answered it with fear, so people kept smoking; (b) phobia, where people know the plane is very unlikely to crash and the emotion stays. She claims emotion-targeted techniques can resolve long-standing problems faster than slow, painful therapy.
   - Maps to the "cognitive component is not the active ingredient" and under-tested theory-of-change reasons. Her speed and efficiency claims are untested here.
4. **She tried optimising and control herself and it backfired.** In her own life, more control made things worse, and attention to small details cost the big things; she dropped it. (Personal anecdote, kept at this level of detail on purpose.)
