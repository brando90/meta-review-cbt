# Claim-by-claim evidence map (Draft 4)

**Doc link:** <https://github.com/brando90/meta-review-cbt/blob/main/review/claim_evidence_map.md>

**TLDR:** The review retains all ten proposed reasons cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) may fail, with executive function and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) as the deepest case study. The new evolutionary, evidence-selection and clinical-formulation claims have separate evidence grades and explicit limits below.

Status key: **S** supported, **P** partly supported, **N** not supported / contradicted, **G** gap in the sources searched, **Contested** credible evidence on both sides. A gap is not proof of absence. Section numbers refer to the current `latex/main.tex` input order. The first two tables retain earlier source checks; the Draft 4 table records the current targeted extension, not a fresh verification of the entire bibliography.

| # | Claim | Key evidence (see paper) | Status | Section |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reappraisal draws on working memory | Load abolishes reappraisal effects in two electrophysiology studies; capacity predicts success; late reappraisal costs self-control. Also: works and is trainable when load is low | S (lab) | 4 |
| 2 | Acute stress impairs prefrontal executive function | Animal work; human meta-analysis: working memory and flexibility impaired, inhibition nuanced | P | 5 |
| 3 | Stress abolishes reappraisal in humans | One clear positive (Raio 2013); five later studies found no group-level impairment; stressors moderate | **Contested** | 5 |
| 4 | 'Prefrontal cortex goes off-line' under anxiety (popular claim) | Phrase traces to a hedged 1998 monkey line, used by its author for uncontrollable stress only; popular applications to worry cite non-worry studies | **N (strong form)** | 5 |
| 5 | Anxiety impairs inhibition and shifting | Group-level meta-analytic support, with qualifiers (older adults null, inhibition not associated in generalized anxiety disorder meta-analysis) | P | 5 |
| 6 | ADHD involves executive weaknesses | Medium effects, heterogeneous; higher prevalence with better measurement | S (with caveat) | 6 |
| 7 | Anxiety adds executive load in ADHD | One careful child study: trait anxiety did not uniquely predict executive function | **N/G** | 6 |
| 8 | CBT works on average | Moderate versus placebo; larger versus waitlist | S | 7 |
| 9 | Effects shrink with active controls and bias adjustment | Several sensitivity analyses support attenuation; Driessen's 25% point-estimate change has an uncertain published–unpublished contrast (details below) | S (qualified) | 7 |
| 10 | Effects shrink over time | Disputed; flat, not declining, in anxiety; depression decline called spurious | **N (as stated)** | 7 |
| 11 | Restructuring is not the active ingredient | Many null component results, often underpowered; positive findings in some disorders and comparisons | **Contested** | 8 |
| 12 | Exposure/behavioural elements carry the benefit | Supported in some component comparisons; exposure may act via expectancy change | P | 8 |
| 13 | Executive function moderates CBT outcome | Overall outcome mostly null; skill-level signal in older adults; opposite direction in two samples | **P (skill-level only)** | 11 |
| 14 | ADHD moderates CBT for anxiety in adults | No adult study identified in the earlier targeted search. Child data: no moderation | **G** | 10, 11 |
| 15 | Mindfulness/acceptance approaches are not established as superior to CBT | Non-significant pooled contrasts do not establish universal equivalence; mindfulness-based stress reduction non-inferior to escitalopram is a different comparison | P | 9 |
| 16 | Adult ADHD CBT works, mostly as an adjunct, with behavioural active ingredient | Modest effects, reduced versus active controls and blinded raters; guidelines adjunct; mechanism inferred, not established | P | 10 |

## Draft 3 additions: reasons beyond executive function (R4-R10)

| # | Claim | Key evidence | Status | Section |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17 | Dropout is a major failure mode of CBT | 15.9% before and 26.2% during treatment (115 studies); 19.7% across all psychotherapy, not moderated by orientation | S (not CBT-specific) | 12 |
| 18 | Fewer than half of patients respond | Fewer than half met response or remission in 92 psychotherapy studies; 48% of care-as-usual also no longer met criteria | S | 12 |
| 19 | Routine care does worse than trials | IAPT 40.3% reliably recovered, 6.6% deteriorated; anxiety effectiveness within efficacy range | P | 12, 14 |
| 20 | Gains erode after CBT ends | 29% relapse at 1 year, 54% at 2 after cognitive therapy; better than stopping medication (OR 2.61) | P | 12 |
| 21 | CBT does harm | Deterioration lower than in controls (5.8% vs 17.4%); 5-20% unwanted events across psychotherapy; harms rarely monitored (21%) | **P (reporting S, harm excess N)** | 12 |
| 22 | Comorbidity, chronicity and adversity limit CBT | Personality disorder OR 2.16, maltreatment OR 1.43, chronic depression d = 0.23; all treatments | P (not CBT-specific) | 13 |
| 23 | CBT fails neurodivergent patients | Autistic adults: 10 RCTs, very uncertain; rater-dependent effects | **G** | 13 |
| 24 | CBT is culturally biased | Adapted CBT g = 0.67 and 0.52 over unadapted; trials exclude linguistic minorities | P | 13 |
| 25 | Access and context drive failure | Waits and missed appointments predict worse IAPT outcomes; service factors 33% of variance; LMIC lay delivery works | S | 13 |
| 26 | Poor delivery explains non-response | Therapist effects about 5%; adherence/competence r = .02/.07 pooled; no causal tests of drift | **P/G** | 14 |
| 27 | Cognitive change mediates CBT benefit | Mixed: precedes sudden gains; reverse direction vs medication; "not yet conclusive" | **Contested** | 15 |
| 28 | Outcome measures and trials are biased upward | Clinician vs self-report delta g = 0.20; 11/115 trials high quality (d 0.22 vs 0.74); selective reporting SMD -0.81 vs -0.54; yet multiverse g = 0.56 | S (certainty, not null) | 15 |

## Draft 4: evolutionary learning, evidence selection and clinical formulation

The effect-size symbols below retain the sources' conventions: `g` is Hedges' standardized mean difference and `r` is a correlation. Brackets are source-reported 95% confidence intervals. There is no new pooled analysis or newly collected participant data; full denominators, reading levels and uncertainty limits are in the linked source notes.

| # | Claim | Checked evidence and boundary | Status | Current placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 29 | Evolutionarily conserved learning makes behavioural techniques clinically superior | Operant learning in *Aplysia* and conserved basal-ganglia organization support biological plausibility, not a treatment comparison; prefrontal evolution is not a unitary old/new split | **N (inference)** | `15-mechanism.tex`, evolutionary subsection |
| 30 | Repetition necessarily produces a habit | Wood's context-dependent account; de Wit's five unsuccessful human habit-induction experiments. Goal-directed and habitual control are interacting, distinguishable constructs | **N (universal claim)** | `08-ingredients.tex`, `15-mechanism.tex` |
| 31 | Cognition only consolidates prior experience | Li and Doll show instruction effects in laboratory learning; Johnco supports processing before or after exposure, with a small, non-significant anxiety difference favouring behavioural experiments; Krause finds no detected timing contrast, not equivalence | **N (universal claim)** | `08-ingredients.tex`, `14-delivery.tex`, `15-mechanism.tex` |
| 32 | Cognitive restructuring adds no benefit | Richards supports non-inferiority of behavioural activation for depression but compares packages and workforces; Sipka's component advantages are time-dependent and limited by attrition; Cuijpers finds a small benefit of full packages (`g=.21 [.03,.39]`) across heterogeneous components | **Contested**, not a neural-mechanism result | `08-ingredients.tex` |
| 33 | Reward revaluation is an established alternative mechanism | Ludwig proposes it; Taylor's uncontrolled eating studies associate model-derived values with behaviour, but the larger sample lacks a corresponding decline in reported eating across tool uses | **G/P (hypothesis, limited process evidence)** | `09-alternatives.tex` |
| 34 | Researcher allegiance and publication selection affect the evidence base | Munder: association across 30 meta-analyses. Driessen: adding six unpublished studies to 20 published control comparisons changes `g=.52 [.37,.68]` to `.39 [.08,.70]`; published–unpublished contrast `p=.07`, not proof of a known 25% bias magnitude | **S (selection), P (magnitude/causal attribution)** | `07-evidence.tex` |
| 35 | CBT-oriented reviewers suppress alternatives | Driessen's 13 unpublished studies: 2 submitted/rejected, 6 not submitted, 5 unclear. Emerson's randomized reviewer-bias evidence is from orthopaedics. Tolin retains some CBT advantages after allegiance adjustment | **G (CBT-specific claim)** | `07-evidence.tex` |
| 36 | Change can target the relationship with feelings rather than their elimination | Hayes defines acceptance/defusion and psychological flexibility; Stockton's 12 mediation studies are generally consistent but methodologically limited | **S (model), P (process), G (unique causality)** | `13-patients.tex` |
| 37 | Four patient types and their prevalence are established | Readiness (76 studies/25,917 patients) and reactance (13 controlled studies/1,208 patients) are relevant but do not validate the informal split or prevalence claims | **G (typology); P (related constructs)** | `13-patients.tex` |
| 38 | Alliance and rupture repair matter | Alliance `r=.278 [.256,.299]` across 295 studies; reciprocal early alliance/symptom relations in 17 studies/5,350 participants; one 331-patient/80-therapist study locates the association at therapist level. Repair `r=.29 [.10,.47]` in 11 studies; training/supervision `r=.11 [-.09,.30]` in six comparisons does not establish benefit | **S (association), unresolved causal magnitude** | `14-delivery.tex` |
| 39 | Feedback/timing make any therapeutic probe effective | A feedback trial with 2,233 patients supports a specific structured intervention for not-on-track patients. Neither this nor alliance evidence directly tests early reframing conditional on alliance readiness | **P (specific feedback), G (broad timing mechanism)** | `14-delivery.tex`, `17-agenda.tex` |
