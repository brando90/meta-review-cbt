# Preliminary points on CBT from a personal communication (Oct 25 2026)

**Status: preliminary; not evidence; not citable.** Source: informal Spanish voice notes received by the owner on Oct 25 2026, from a clinician trained in strategic therapy. They are claims, not findings, and had not been checked against the literature when this file was written. Do not cite them as evidence and do not name the source in the manuscript.

## Points, paraphrased

1. **Confounds in CBT effect studies.** Many confounds, above all therapy time and contact: being able to vent and talk helps regardless of technique.
   - Test against: attention-placebo and time-matched comparator trials, dose and common-factor analyses.
2. **Strategic therapy: the attempted solution is the problem.** What a person does to solve a problem can perpetuate it ("the solution becomes the problem"). Example: trying to force sleep keeps sleep away. Strategic therapy works by interrupting the attempted solution.
   - Test against: paradoxical-intention and sleep-effort literature, thought-suppression and avoidance literature, outcome studies of brief strategic therapy.
3. **Reason does not change behaviour; emotion does.** People know what to eat, or that a phobic fear is very unlikely, and still act otherwise. The strategic approach works with four emotions (horror, pleasure, fear, rage). Examples: smoking (graphic warning images answered pleasure with fear and people kept smoking) and phobia. The claim that emotion-targeted techniques resolve long-standing problems faster than slow therapy is untested here.
   - Test against: intention-behaviour gap, fear-appeal and warning-label studies, exposure versus cognitive-only treatment.
4. **Personal experience of over-control.** Trying to optimise and control one's own health backfired; the person dropped it. Anecdote only, kept at this level on purpose.
