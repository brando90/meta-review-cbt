\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
This is a critical theoretical review, not a systematic review reported in full under the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines.
We select literatures relevant to ten reasons CBT may fail, with executive function and adult ADHD as the deepest case study.
The initial searches ran in October 2026 across PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, publisher pages and reference lists of key meta-analyses.
They covered efficacy and bias; components and mechanisms; executive function and stress; ADHD; non-response, dropout, relapse and harm; patient and context factors; delivery; and theory and measurement.
We prioritised meta-analyses, component trials, moderator studies, and critiques and defences of CBT.
A targeted extension in November 2026 examined evolutionary learning, researcher allegiance and publication selection, acceptance, engagement, alliance and intervention timing.
This extension followed proposed claims and relevant counterevidence; it was not an exhaustive update of every literature.

\paragraph{Verification}
For the extension, we checked the title, authors and publication year of scientific references against DOI-resolved Crossref records.
We distinguished this bibliographic check from reading abstracts or relevant full-text passages to verify findings.
Quoted numbers came from those passages, not from search summaries or memory.
The public source notes record reading levels, scope limits, inaccessible candidates and retrieval provenance; copyrighted reading copies remain outside the public repository.
Earlier source notes and the public verification log document prior DOI checks and abstract reading; their partial-verification flags remain in place.
Popular sources motivate questions but do not establish empirical findings.

\paragraph{Limitations of the method}
Selection was not independently duplicated, the review was not preregistered, and we did not formally assess the risk of bias of every included review.
Our synthesis therefore remains vulnerable to selection and interpretation bias, including our own preference for the proposed argument.
Evidence from depression is used where anxiety evidence is thin, with the distinction stated.
A structured umbrella search with independent screening and risk-of-bias assessment remains necessary before claiming systematic coverage (Section~\ref{sec:agenda}).
