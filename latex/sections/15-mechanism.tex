\section{Theory, mechanism and measurement critiques}\label{sec:mechanism}
\paragraph{Mechanisms are under-tested} \citet{kazdin2009understanding} argues that ``there is no evidence-based explanation of how or why even the most well-studied interventions produce change'', and sets standards (temporal precedence, specificity, dose--response, experiment; \citealp{kraemer2002mediators}) that most CBT mediation studies do not meet.
Positive evidence exists: sudden symptom gains were preceded by cognitive change in two datasets \citep{tang1999sudden,tang2005cognitive}.
It is mixed: dysfunctional-attitude change mediated symptom reduction in CBT versus interpersonal therapy, but the reverse direction fit CBT versus medication ($n=130$; \citealp{quilty2008mediational}); the CBT-side reviews call the evidence ``not yet conclusive'' \citep{driessen2010mediators} and the evidence for skill use as a mediator preliminary \citep{hundt2013skills}.
Cognitive vulnerability--stress interactions predicted later depression only weakly in youth \citep{lakdawalla2007cognitive}.
Defences of the cognitive model and its updates are in \citet{beck2014generic} and \citet{hofmann2013science}.

\paragraph{Is the cognitive part the active part?} In a network meta-analysis of 45 trials ($n=3{,}382$), cognitive restructuring, behavioural activation and full CBT did not differ, and all beat usual care (SMD 0.57, 0.52, 0.44) \citep{ciharova2021restructuring}.
This is consistent with a behavioural reading and equally with a cognitive one; see Section~\ref{sec:ingredients} for the dismantling trials.
For exposure, inhibitory learning emphasises new learning through expectancy violation \citep{vervliet2013extinction,weisman2018augmentation}.
In this framework, new associations can compete with earlier threat learning rather than erase it \citep{craske2014maximizing}.
This gives a learning-based account of a corrective experience, not evidence that emotional intensity itself is therapeutic.
It is not necessarily an alternative to belief change: revising an expectancy can itself constitute cognitive change, even without explicit restructuring \citep{hofmann2008extinction}.
A large trial found expectancy-violation-based exposure effective and optimisable \citep{pittig2021intensified}.
Attempts to suppress unwanted thoughts can produce a later rebound \citep{wenzlaff2000suppression,abramowitz2001suppression}.
The meta-analysis found a small-to-moderate effect that varied with the target thought and its measurement, with few clinical samples \citep{abramowitz2001suppression}.
This concerns suppression and should not be treated as evidence that cognitive restructuring causes rebound.
Experiential avoidance gives a third-wave counter-theory \citep{hayes1996avoidance}, though a key measure may largely track distress \citep{wolgast2014aaq} and third-wave trials were less stringent than CBT trials \citep{ost2008thirdwave}.
The process-based critique of one protocol per diagnosis \citep{hayes2021processbased,hofmann2019process} comes from advocates of the alternative.

\paragraph{Communication format is not a mechanism} Communication format---for example, verbal discussion versus an action-based exercise---does not identify the process responsible for change.
The clinical perspective proposes that a verbal reframe can help a person experience a different response, rather than merely communicate an explanation.
Conversely, behavioural experiments can change expectancies, as in the public-speaking trial (Section~\ref{sec:ingredients}).
An experience-based description does not establish an exclusively subcortical mechanism.
Nor do the component and timing comparisons directly validate brief strategic therapy as a treatment package.

\paragraph{Common factors} The alliance and therapist effects reviewed in Section~\ref{sec:delivery}, together with expectancy ($r=.18$; \citealp{constantino2018expectation}), are replicable and modest.
\citet{wampold2015common} argues they outweigh specific ingredients, and \citet{enck2019placebo} frame context effects in psychotherapy; this is contested, and it explains why trials without blinding cannot cleanly isolate CBT's specific effect.

\paragraph{Measurement} Clinician-rated scales show larger effects than self-report ($\Delta g=0.20$; \citealp{cuijpers2010selfreport}).
Seven depression scales share little symptom content (mean overlap 0.36; \citealp{fried2017symptoms}); four common scales lack unidimensionality and longitudinal invariance ($n=3{,}509$; \citealp{fried2016invariance}), and sum scores hide which symptoms change \citep{fried2015sumscores}.
Response is therefore partly instrument-dependent, and standardised, patient-defined outcomes are rare \citep{cuijpers2019targets,lambert2009clinical}.

\paragraph{Evidence quality} Only 11 of 115 depression trials met eight quality criteria, with $d=0.22$ versus 0.74 in the rest \citep{cuijpers2009quality}; excess significant findings and mean power of 49\% suggest small-study bias \citep{flint2014excess}; only 15\% of audited trials were prospectively registered \citep{cybulski2016registration}; and selective reporting inflated effects (SMD $-0.81$ versus $-0.54$; \citealp{miguel2021selective}).
A Cochrane-method review rated all eight cognitive-therapy-versus-usual-care trials at high risk of bias \citep{jakobsen2011cttau}, though risk-of-bias tools fit psychotherapy awkwardly \citep{munder2018rob}.
Fairness requires the counterweight: a multiverse analysis of 4,281 meta-analyses still gave a mean $g=0.56$, 90\% clinically relevant, though pooling all psychotherapies \citep{plessen2023multiverse}.
Field-level critiques call for mechanism research and less redundant trials \citep{cuijpers2015future,tackett2019replication}.

\paragraph{Reading} R10 and the measurement and bias part of R3 identify limits on certainty.
These findings do not establish a single mechanism of CBT-specific failure.
They show that causal accounts of change remain under-tested and that trial selection and measurement can inflate estimated effects.

\subsection{Evolutionary learning: motivation, not a treatment ranking}\label{sec:learning}
The evolutionary argument begins with a defensible observation: learning from consequences does not require human verbal reasoning.
Operant reward learning has been demonstrated in the mollusc \emph{Aplysia} \citep{brembs2002operant}.
Comparative work also identifies conserved basal-ganglia organization across lamprey and mammals \citep{grillner2016basal}.
Neither observation compares psychotherapies or establishes that an older mechanism produces greater clinical benefit.
The prefrontal cortex is not a single recent human addition: its subdivisions include shared mammalian regions and later primate specializations \citep{preuss2022evolution}.
Evolutionary conservation supplies biological plausibility, not an ordering of treatment efficacy.

The learning concepts also require separation.
\emph{Instrumental learning} concerns relations between actions and their consequences; \emph{Pavlovian learning} concerns predictive relations between events \citep{dolan2013goals}.
Within instrumental control, goal-directed action is sensitive to current outcome value, whereas habitual control is relatively insensitive to outcome devaluation.
These are interacting forms of control, not a literal division between a cognitive cortex and a non-cognitive basal ganglia \citep{dolan2013goals}.
Repeating an action in a stable context can support habit formation, but repetition is not itself a separate learning system \citep{wood2016habit}.
Five unsuccessful human habit-induction experiments caution against assuming that practice necessarily yields outcome-insensitive responding \citep{dewit2018failures}.
Reward-related motivation is also not identical to conscious pleasure: wanting and liking can dissociate \citep{berridge2016liking}.

The useful clinical hypothesis is therefore about the relation between understanding and action, not the age of a brain region.
For persistent avoidance under high executive demand, structured behavioural practice may produce more durable functional change than restructuring without comparable practice.
This proposed comparison concerns acquisition, generalization and cognitive cost; it is not an established treatment ranking.
Nor does it require that an unpleasant feeling disappear before action changes, as the acceptance-based formulation makes explicit (Section~\ref{sec:clinicalformulation}).

The stronger claim that cognition can only consolidate experience is contradicted by laboratory evidence.
Instructed reward probabilities improved decisions in one learning task \citep{li2011instructed}.
Misleading instructions biased choices despite contrary experience in another \citep{doll2009instruction}.
These findings show that instructions can influence learning and behaviour, not that cognitive restructuring is invariably useful in clinical care.
The distinction between communication format and mechanism also permits cognitive preparation for behavioural practice.
The exposure-component and timing evidence therefore constrains any universal ``experience first'' claim (Sections~\ref{sec:ingredients} and~\ref{sec:delivery}).
The defensible research question is which combination of practice, cognitive processing and relational support works for a specified problem and patient, not which system is evolutionarily older.
