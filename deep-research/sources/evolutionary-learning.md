# Evolutionary learning: source verification and claim limits

**Doc link:** <https://github.com/brando90/meta-review-cbt/blob/main/deep-research/sources/evolutionary-learning.md>

**TLDR:** Comparative neuroscience supports ancient learning capacities, not the clinical superiority of behavioural over cognitive techniques. Instructions, cognitive processing during exposure, null timing contrasts and uncertain habit induction constrain the proposed experience-first account.

Checked: 11-08-2026. Scope: targeted extension, not an exhaustive review. ## Definitions and comparative evidence

| `dolan2013goals` / [10.1016/j.neuron.2013.09.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.09.007) | Full-text instrumental/habit definitions, neural-system interactions, computational and Pavlovian sections | Goal-directed control is sensitive to outcome value and contingency; habitual control is relatively outcome-insensitive. ## Components and timing

- **`jacobson1996component`**, [10.1037/0022-006X.64.2.295](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-006X.64.2.295), **abstract read**: 150 depression outpatients, behavioural activation, activation plus automatic-thought skills, and full cognitive therapy; no detected advantage for full treatment at termination or six months. Negative thinking changed in the behavioural condition too. A null difference is not a formal equivalence result.
- **`richards2016cobra`**, [10.1016/S0140-6736(16)31140-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(16)31140-0), **full-text methods and primary results read**: 440 adults randomized, 221 to behavioural activation and 219 to CBT. At 12 months, 175 and 189 respectively were assessed in the modified intention-to-treat analysis; 135 and 151 in the per-protocol analysis. The adjusted CBT-minus-activation PHQ-9 difference was `0.1 [-1.3, 1.5]` (95% confidence interval) and `0.0 [-1.5, 1.6]` respectively. The source's non-inferiority margin was 1.9 scale points, judged against the lower interval bound of −1.9. The reported `p=.89`/`.99` values concern the between-group contrast, **not** proof of non-inferiority from non-significance. Behavioural activation was delivered by junior mental health workers, and CBT by psychological therapists. The pragmatic comparison therefore changes both package and workforce; it does not isolate a single component. No inference of a specific neural mechanism.
- **`cuijpers2017components`**, [10.1080/10503307.2017.1395922](https://doi.org/10.1080/10503307.2017.1395922), **abstract read**: 16 studies, 22 comparisons, 15 components; full treatment versus removal of a component gave Hedges' `g=.21 [.03,.39]`. Only one study had low risk of bias; all but one lacked power for effects smaller than `g=.55`. The powered study added emotion-regulation skills, not a general estimate of restructuring's contribution. Correct issue year to **2019** (online 2017), keeping the stable bibliography key.
- **`sipka2025factorial`**, [10.1159/000542425](https://doi.org/10.1159/000542425), **full-text design, component descriptions and outcomes read**: 464 adults with diagnosed social anxiety disorder, 16 combinations of four components. Psychoeducation and exposure improved post-treatment outcomes relative to conditions without them; restructuring/attention additions did not establish superiority. The psychoeducation/exposure contrasts were no longer significant four months after post-treatment; conditions with restructuring/attention training improved further between post-treatment and follow-up. The limitations section reports 34% pre-to-post and 56% post-to-follow-up dropout, limiting the follow-up inference. Exposure included explicit expectations and comparison with experienced outcomes. The finding is not a cognition-free treatment effect or proof of equivalence at follow-up.
- **`craske2014maximizing`**, [10.1016/j.brat.2014.04.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brat.2014.04.006), **abstract read**: inhibitory-learning framework and proposed exposure-optimization strategies, including expectancy violation. A clinical/mechanistic framework, not universal proof that restructuring before exposure harms outcome.
- **`hofmann2008extinction`**, [10.1016/j.cpr.2007.04.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2007.04.009), **abstract read**: review interprets exposure as changing harm expectancies. Keep the cognitive-mechanism interpretation as a serious alternative; procedure and mediator are not the same thing.
- **`krause2022claustrophobia`**, [10.1177/01454455221075754](https://doi.org/10.1177/01454455221075754), **full-text methods/results/discussion passages read**: 93 randomized, 90 completed questionnaire follow-up; 88 in behavioural-approach analyses after protocol exclusions. Restructuring-before and consolidation-after conditions showed no detected outcome difference at post-treatment/one month. The thought-record and consolidation exercises differed, so sequence was not the sole manipulation. Functional-impairment diagnostic criterion was optional; call the sample claustrophobic fear rather than assuming everyone had a fully diagnosed disorder. No equivalence claim.
- **`johnco2025restructuring`**, [10.1037/ccp0000953](https://doi.org/10.1037/ccp0000953), **abstract read**: 249 adults with elevated public-speaking anxiety, single intensive session and one-week follow-up. Behavioural experiments and restructuring-before-exposure both improved anxiety more than exposure without explicit expectancy processing; the small, non-significant anxiety difference favoured behavioural experiments, which also produced greater expectancy change in a shorter session. The abstract does not supply an interval for that comparison. Do not report equivalence, durable superiority or a clinically diagnosed sample.

## Additional candidates checked but not newly cited

- `yin2006habits` / [10.1038/nrn1919](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn1919), abstract: goal-directed/habitual-control review; covered by Dolan & Dayan in the new prose.
- `yin2004lesions` / [10.1111/j.1460-9568.2004.03095.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1460-9568.2004.03095.x), abstract: rat dorsolateral-striatal lesions preserved outcome sensitivity under habit-inducing training. Not proof that human clinical avoidance is a habit.
- `schultz1997reward` / [10.1126/science.275.5306.1593](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593), abstract: dopamine and reward-prediction errors; does not equate dopamine with pleasure.
- `longmore2007challenge` / [10.1016/j.cpr.2006.08.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2006.08.001), abstract: older critical review of cognitive components; retain newer component uncertainty rather than relying on this narrative review alone.

## Interpretation and measurement boundaries

No new effect sizes were pooled or computed. All study numbers above are transcribed from the cited sources; intervals are source-reported 95% intervals, not new bootstraps. Missing intervals remain unavailable, not inferred. This review has no newly collected participant data or train/validation/test split. Lab findings, observational process studies, clinical component trials and theoretical proposals remain separate evidence classes.

**TLDR-end:** [meta-review-cbt: evolutionary-learning evidence] The source-checked extension supports testing practice and learning under specific conditions, not inferring treatment superiority from evolution. Cognitive counterexamples and the negative/uncertain findings remain part of the argument.
