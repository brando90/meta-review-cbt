# Evidence selection: allegiance, publication and peer review

**Doc link:** <https://github.com/brando90/meta-review-cbt/blob/main/deep-research/sources/evidence-selection.md>

**TLDR:** Researcher allegiance and selective availability of results limit confidence in psychotherapy estimates. These findings do not establish that CBT-oriented reviewers suppress alternatives, and recovered unpublished trials show why non-submission must be separated from journal rejection.

## Driessen et al. (2015): direct recovered-study evidence

**Key:** `driessen2015publicationbias`; [10.1371/journal.pone.0137864](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0137864). **Read:** full-text methods, results, Table 2, “Reasons for non-publication” and discussion/limitations. Scope: US National Institutes of Health-funded psychological-treatment trials for major depressive disorder, grant years 1972–2008. Not CBT-only and not anxiety.

- Fifty-seven grants met inclusion criteria; two trials never started. Among the **55 initiated**, 42 led to publication and **13 did not** (23.6%; source-reported binomial 95% interval 13–37%). Multiple grants funding one study were counted together; some grants produced multiple published trials.
- The authors recovered data from **11 of 13** unpublished studies; those data were not all relevant to every comparator analysis. The control comparison used **six unpublished** and **20 published** studies, not all 55 grants.
- Published control comparisons: Hedges' standardized mean difference `g=.52 [.37,.68]`, 20 studies, 1,767 participants. Unpublished: `g=.20 [-.11,.51]`, six studies, 336 participants. Combined: `g=.39 [.08,.70]`, 26 studies, 2,103 participants. These are source-reported random-effects 95% confidence intervals, not recalculated estimates.
- The source reports a **25% lower point estimate** after adding unpublished data. The test of the published–unpublished contrast was `Q_between=3.34`, one degree of freedom, `p=.07` (null: equal subgroup effects). Do not describe the point-estimate decrease as a precise, statistically established universal correction. The combined effect against controls remained positive.
- Of the **13 unpublished** studies, **two were submitted and rejected**, **six were never submitted**, and **five had unclear submission status**. Report these three categories intact. Reviewer preference cannot be inferred from non-publication alone.
- Remaining limitations: unavailable data from two studies, inability to quantify outcome-reporting bias, restricted funding/country/diagnostic scope, weak primary-study quality, and few unpublished studies per contrast.

## Inference boundary for the manuscript

A count of studies is not an effect-size comparison. Treatment-package efficacy does not identify the causal contribution of cognitive restructuring. Researcher preference, publication selection, peer-review result preference and CBT-specific reviewer allegiance are separate constructs. No direct estimate of the last construct was identified in the eight checked candidates; this is a statement about this extension, not proof that no such study exists.

No new pooled estimate or statistical test was calculated. Counts, effects, intervals and p-values above are transcribed from sources; unavailable uncertainty is explicitly noted. The manuscript retains positive CBT evidence and applies the same bias concerns to alternative orientations and to this review's own selection process.

**TLDR-end:** [meta-review-cbt: evidence-selection evidence] The verified critique is about confidence in particular evidence streams, not a demonstrated CBT-reviewer explanation of the field. Non-submission, researcher allegiance and positive-outcome review bias must not be conflated.
