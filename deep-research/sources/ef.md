# Executive function, stress and emotion regulation: literature slice for the CBT/anxiety/ADHD meta-review

Scope: does executive function (EF) predict CBT uptake or outcome; does stress degrade prefrontal control and cognitive reappraisal; attentional control theory; 2020-2026 work; the "prefrontal cortex goes offline" popularisation. All quotes are copied from abstracts read via Europe PMC, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar or PubMed. Every DOI was confirmed against Crossref with bibfetch.py. Entries are in `ef.bib` (97 entries: 94 VERIFIED, 3 PARTIAL).

## Synthesis (10 lines)

1. EF predicts skill quality more reliably than outcome. In older adults, poorer cognitive flexibility predicts poorer cognitive-restructuring (CR) quality and less distress reduction from CR (johnco2013flexibility, johnco2014flexibility, johnco2015clinical, mohlman2013executive). This supports the thesis at the level of the skill.
2. The same studies show weak or no prediction of overall CBT outcome: "Pre-treatment cognitive flexibility was not related to ... overall treatment outcome" (johnco2014flexibility). Null or mixed results also appear for adult OCD (vandborg2016memory), child anxiety (godovich2020executive) and brief psychotherapy for later-life depression (schurr2025executive). The thesis cannot lean on "low EF means CBT fails".
3. Some results run the opposite way: in pediatric OCD, better EF test performance raised non-response to exposure-based CBT (hybel2017executive), and in late-life depression worse WCST performance went with better CBT response (goodkind2016executive). CBT appears to work by routes other than CR, which helps CBT's defenders and complicates the thesis.
4. Where EF matters for treatment, structured and externalised therapies seem to help. Problem-solving therapy beat supportive therapy in EF-impaired older adults, and the advantage was larger with more cognitive impairment (arean2010problem, alexopoulos2011disability). This points to adapting CBT, not abandoning it.
5. Mechanism side: acute uncontrollable stress impairs PFC-dependent working memory in monkeys and rodents (arnsten1998noise, arnsten2009stress, arnsten2015stress). In humans a meta-analysis finds acute stress impairs working memory and cognitive flexibility, with nuanced effects on inhibition and many moderators (shields2016stress). Human imaging shows reallocation of resources to salience networks, not a switch-off (hermans2014networks).
6. Reappraisal under stress is contested. Raio 2013 found stressed participants showed no fear reduction after regulation training (raio2013stress). Five later studies (n = 54 to 98) found no group-level impairment (shermohammed2017stress, sandner2021withstands, langer2021delayed, langer2023rapid, hamza2024acceptance), although two reported sex- or cortisol-linked effects. The lab stressors were moderate, so these null results may not transfer to clinical-level stress, and that gap is itself an open question.
7. Reappraisal does draw on working memory in the lab. High load eliminated the reappraisal effect on the LPP in two ERP studies (gan2017load, adamczyk2022load), capacity predicted reappraisal success (schmeichel2008memory), and late-onset reappraisal costs self-control resources (sheppes2008costs). The same lab literature shows reappraisal works well in unloaded, instructed conditions (hofmann2009anxiety, webb2012feeling) and can be trained (denny2014training).
8. Attentional control theory is supported at the group level (shi2019attentional g = -0.58; moran2016anxiety g = -.334) but with qualifiers: deficits mainly in efficiency, mainly under load, absent in older GAD, and working memory not related to social anxiety (harrison2025executive). One study found enhanced control over negative distractors in GAD (hallion2019enhanced).
9. The "offline" phrase traces to a hedged line in a 1998 monkey study ("Stress may take the PFC 'off-line'", arnsten1998noise) and is still used in scare quotes by its author for "uncontrollable stress" (arnsten2020guanfacine). Brewer and Roy 2021 apply it to worry and cite Arnsten 2009 and 2015, which are not worry studies (brewer2021habit). Their app trial (n = 65, treatment as usual as control) did not test any prefrontal mechanism (roy2021digital).
10. Fair summary for CBT: the evidence that CR is EF-costly and stress-fragile is plausible and partly supported, but direct tests of "EF moderates CBT outcome" are mostly small, heterogeneous or null, and CBT has several ingredients besides CR. The thesis is better framed as "CR is the most EF-dependent component, so delivery should be scaffolded" than as "CBT fails when EF is low".

## Method notes and caveats

- VERIFIED = DOI resolves in Crossref, title and authors match, and abstract read in full. PARTIAL = existence confirmed but abstract not (fully) read.
- Numbers are quoted only from abstracts I read. Where the abstract has no effect size I say so.
- Year follows Europe PMC print year (for example vandborg2016memory, goodkind2016executive, buhle2014reappraisal and bishop2009trait have Crossref online-first years one year earlier).
- Interpretive comments ("bears on thesis") are mine, not the authors'.

Critical points on the "offline" idea (my synthesis from the verified abstracts):
1. Species: the molecular and "offline" claims come from rodents and monkeys (arnsten1998noise, arnsten2009stress, arnsten2015stress, joyce2025stress). Human evidence is indirect (behaviour, fMRI).
2. Controllability: the effect is described for uncontrollable stress (arnsten2009stress, arnsten2015stress, arnsten2020guanfacine), and mPFC is itself engaged by controllable stress (amat2005prefrontal, maier2016helplessness).
3. Human effects are partial, not a shutdown: reduced DLPFC working-memory activity under moderate stress in 27 women (qin2009stress), reallocation then recovery (hermans2014networks), stress activating inferior frontal cortex (berretz2021brain), and moderated, mixed behavioural effects (shields2016stress, plieger2020stress, starcke2012decision).
4. Reappraisal-specific tests are mixed (raio2013stress versus shermohammed2017stress, sandner2021withstands, langer2021delayed, langer2023rapid, hamza2024acceptance).
5. Worry or anxiety as the trigger of PFC loss is not tested in the Arnsten papers; the nearest human evidence is anxiety-linked attentional control deficits (shi2019attentional, moran2016anxiety, bishop2009trait), which are moderate and load-dependent.
6. Brewer's wider claims ("completely offline", "willpower is seated in the PFC") appear only in web interviews and book summaries whose text I did not open; treated as UNVERIFIED and not used.
