# Mechanisms and component evidence: literature slice notes

Scope: dismantling/component studies, mediation, exposure and inhibitory learning, third-wave alternatives, 2020-2026 work.
Companion file: `/var/lib/agent/scratch/at735/lit/mechanisms.bib` (93 entries, every DOI resolved in Crossref via bibfetch.py and title/authors/year compared with expectation).
Line format: `bibkey | DOI | design | headline numbers (quoted from abstract) | bearing on thesis | status`.
Status: VERIFIED = abstract read (Europe PMC, PubMed or OpenAlex) and quoted figures taken from it. PARTIAL = paper exists and metadata confirmed, but abstract only seen in part or via a secondary summary; no verbatim claims should be built on the flagged parts.

## Synthesis (read this first)

1. Component evidence in anxiety is thin and mostly null for cognitive restructuring (CR) as an *add-on*: Norton & Price (108 trials) found "no differential efficacy for any treatment components"; Pompoli's panic component NMA (72 studies) singled out interoceptive exposure, not CR; the 2025 full factorial trial in social anxiety (n=464) found psychoeducation and exposure active, CR and attention training not superior at post-test.
2. It is not clean evidence *against* cognitive methods. Ougrin (2011) found CT favoured over exposure in social phobia (though a 2012 correction re-analysed it and the data were partly mis-entered); Hofmann (2004) found continued gains to 6-month follow-up only after CBT, not exposure alone; Bryant (2008) found adding CR to exposure gave the best PTSD outcome; Resick (2008) found cognitive-only CPT beat written accounts. The thesis should say "no consistent added value", not "no value".
3. The dichotomy is blurry. Hofmann (2008) argues exposure is itself a cognitive intervention (change in expectancy of harm); Johnco (2025, n=249) found threat-expectancy change predicted anxiety reduction and that CR-before-exposure worked about as well as behavioural experiments (small, non-significant edge for experiments); Krause (2022) found order of CR and exposure did not matter. Clark-Wells CT, the top-ranked social anxiety protocol (Sun 2025), runs on behavioural experiments.
4. Mediation evidence is correlational and weak on temporal precedence. Kazdin (2007) and Cuijpers (2019) say no mechanism meets causal criteria; Burns & Spangler and Vittengl/Jarrett found little support for cognitive mediation of symptom change (median r = .06). But Lorenzo-Luaces (2015) and Cristea (2015) conclude cognitive change tracks symptom change however it is achieved, i.e. cognitive change is real but does not show that *restructuring* causes it. Anxiety-specific mediation reviews (PTSD, child anxiety) find negative cognitions are the most consistent mediator, with temporal precedence tested in a minority of studies.
5. Exposure has the strongest direct support for specific phobia (Wolitzky-Taylor), panic (interoceptive exposure), OCD (ERP, with CR alone similar on 3 comparisons) and PTSD (PE equal to CPT, small advantage in a 916-patient trial). The inhibitory-learning programme (Craske 2008, 2014, 2022) gives a mechanism that does not rely on persuasion, but its clinical superiority over standard exposure is not established by the papers read here (Craske papers are reviews or conceptual; Deacon 2013 is a single-session analogue trial).
6. Efficacy magnitude is moderate once controls are fair: Cuijpers 2016: g = 0.75 to 0.88 overall but only 17.4% of trials high quality and over 80% of anxiety trials used waiting lists; Carpenter 2018 placebo-controlled g = 0.56; Hofmann & Smits OR 4.06 vs placebo. Fits "real but moderate".
7. Third-wave and mindfulness approaches match CBT rather than beat it: ACT vs CBT n.s. (A-Tjak p = .140; Oest d = 0.16 n.s., and "not yet well-established for any disorder"); MBT vs CBT g = -0.07 (Khoury); MBSR non-inferior to escitalopram (Hoge 2023). Metacognitive therapy looks better than CBT in small, allegiance-laden trials (Nordahl 2018 recovery 65% vs 38%). None of this proves restructuring is unnecessary, but it shows a range of non-persuasion methods reach similar outcomes.
8. Execution-demand premise has support from basic work, not from treatment trials: anxiety reduces working-memory capacity (Moran 2016, g = -.334, 177 samples), acute stress abolished benefit of a trained reappraisal strategy (Raio 2013), and in one small pilot older GAD patients with persistently low executive function did not respond to CBT (Mohlman 2005). No trial read here tests whether executive function moderates CR vs exposure specifically. This is the thesis's weakest empirical link and should be framed as a hypothesis. ADHD-specific evidence is outside this slice.
9. Most dismantling designs are underpowered: Cuijpers 2019 (16 component studies, pooled full-vs-reduced g = 0.21) says they lack "the statistical power nor the quality to draw any meaningful conclusion". Absence of evidence for CR's added value is therefore weak evidence of absence.
10. Depression studies (Jacobson, Dimidjian, COBRA, Cuijpers 2007) are the best-known "cognitive component adds nothing" results but are indirect for anxiety; use them as supporting analogy only.

## C. Third-wave alternatives

### C1. ACT

hayes2006act | 10.1016/j.brat.2005.06.006 | Narrative review of ACT model and evidence | evidence "broadly supportive, but the literature is not mature"; ACT "works through different processes than active treatment comparisons, including traditional Cognitive-Behavior Therapy" (authors' own claim); "not enough well-controlled studies to conclude that ACT is generally more effective" | complicates (developer's review; mechanism claim contested by Arch & Craske) | VERIFIED
