# Screen of slices D2 (adult-ADHD CBT reviews) and E1 (ADHD x executive function x CBT), 2026-10-25

**Doc link:** <https://github.com/brando90/meta-review-cbt/blob/main/evidence-screen/data/d2-e1-screen-2026-10-25.md>

**TLDR:** None of the 9 E1 records tests baseline executive function as a moderator of CBT; executive function appears only as a secondary outcome. D2 returned 27 records: 7 off-topic, 11 already in the bibliography, 9 new candidates. One agent, Europe PMC only, titles and abstracts only; not a systematic screen.

## Search

Both queries are the frozen wording from `deep-research/search/counts-2026-10-21.tsv`, rerun on 2026-10-25 with abstracts (`resultType=core`). Counts reproduced exactly: D2 = 27, E1 = 9. Script, receipt and raw responses: `search-runs/d2-e1-2026-10-25/`. Twelve DOIs new to the repo resolve at Crossref (`crossref-check.json`).

| Champ et al. 2025, doi 10.2196/69943 | Protocol-style paper, self-determination-theory intervention | Argues CBT rests on a deficit view of executive function; no CBT moderator data |
| Lovakumari & Dwivedi 2026, doi 10.31234/osf.io/9seub_v1 | Preprint, quasi-experimental, group CBT, India | Outcome only; preprint, not peer reviewed |
| Vacher et al. 2022 (doi 10.1186/s13063-022-05996-5) and its 2021 preprint | Protocol, CBT for aggression in children | Child sample; protocol only |

Reading: executive function is measured as something CBT is supposed to improve, never as a baseline variable that predicts who responds. This supports the manuscript's "no study of baseline executive function as a moderator of CBT in adult ADHD" as worded. It holds for these 9 only: the search needs "moderator", "predictor" or "outcome" in the abstract, and "outcome" is in nearly every trial, so the slice is wide and still found no moderator test. A different database or wording (for example "baseline executive function" with "treatment response") is not yet run.
